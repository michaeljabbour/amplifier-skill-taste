Stop refining the deck for now. Propose a **beginner-first practice round** at the next evening: every beginner handles a tool before the repairers demonstrate anything.

Split the ten beginners into two groups of five, one at each end of the table with an experienced repairer. Using tools and safe practice items already available, each beginner gets a short turn doing one simple action—such as tightening a screw on an unplugged, non-hazardous item. The repairer coaches with words and takes over only for safety; the other beginners watch until their turns. Keep powered equipment and hazardous repairs out of this round.

Reserve the first 20 minutes for this. The useful check is simple: **did all ten beginners personally use a tool?** That will tell you more about fixing the participation problem than another round of slide edits.
