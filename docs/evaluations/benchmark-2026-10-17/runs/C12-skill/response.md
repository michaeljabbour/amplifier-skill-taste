The draft’s credibility is the first issue: “40%” and “scientifically proven” fail the evidence check because you have no timing study. “Best-in-class” also has no comparison behind it. Two teammates liking the appearance is useful internal feedback, but it does not establish household usefulness.

Your stated aim is friendly and useful. To my ear, “taste and elegance” makes readers evaluate your design credentials instead of picturing how the list fits their day. I’d shift from claiming superiority to describing a concrete use:

> **One shopping list for your household.**  
> Keep what you need in one shared place. We’re building a prototype and would love to learn what makes shopping together easier.

This assumes the prototype supports a shared list; verify that before using the wording. The revision preserves the household coordination idea and makes its early stage clear. Its warmth is a proposal, not tested audience feedback; the tradeoff is less promotional punch.

A useful next check: show the original and revision to a few people outside the team and ask, “What would you expect this product to do?” That can test clarity and credibility; a separate timing study would be needed for a quantified time-saving claim. Keep the concrete shared-list promise unless that feedback or the prototype’s actual behavior calls for a change.
